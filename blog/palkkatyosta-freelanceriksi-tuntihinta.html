From Employment to Freelancing: How to Calculate an IT Consultant's Minimum Hourly Rate

When you move from employment to independent consulting, it is easy to divide your former monthly salary by 150 hours. That calculation overlooks holidays, costs, insurance and gaps between assignments. Here is how to do it properly.

For experienced IT specialists and architects in Finland, becoming an independent consultant is an attractive option. The first question when joining a partner network or meeting a direct customer is often: “What is your hourly rate?”

If you pick a figure without doing the maths, your company may end up making a loss—or you may earn less than you did as an employee.

1. How many billable hours are there really in a year?

There are approximately 252 working days in a year (1,890 hours). A freelancer cannot bill for all of that time. Deduct the essentials:

  • Annual leave: 25 days (5 weeks) = −187.5 hours
  • Public holidays: approximately 10 days = −75 hours
  • Sick leave reserve: 5 days = −37.5 hours
  • Training and certifications: 7 days = −52.5 hours
  • Administration, sales and accounting: approximately 10–15% of your time

A realistic annual billable total for a full-time consultant is typically 1,250–1,450 hours (a 70–80% utilisation rate).

2. What costs does the company have?

As a freelancer, your company has fixed and variable costs that must be covered before you pay yourself:

  • Entrepreneur’s pension insurance: statutory cover based on the entrepreneur’s confirmed income (typically €6,000–€12,000 per year).
  • Accounting: approximately €100–€180 per month, or €1,500–€2,200 per year.
  • Equipment and licences: computer, displays, phone, Microsoft 365, development tools and GitHub, approximately €3,000–€5,000 per year.
  • Professional liability insurance: consultant’s liability and legal expenses cover, approximately €800–€1,500 per year.
  • Training and certification exams: approximately €1,000–€2,000 per year.

3. The minimum hourly rate formula

The formula is simple:

Minimum hourly rate = (Target gross salary + employer costs + fixed costs + company reserve) / annual billable hours

Example:

  • Target gross income: €75,000 per year (a good senior-level salary)
  • Company costs, pension insurance and insurance: €22,000 per year
  • Company reserve for gaps between assignments: €15,000
  • Total cash requirement: €112,000 per year
  • Billable hours: 1,350 per year

The absolute minimum hourly rate is therefore €112,000 / 1,350 hours = €83/hour (plus VAT).

Market rates and pricing range

In the Finnish market, assignments for senior Azure architects and cloud specialists typically fall between €85 and €125/hour (plus VAT). The exact rate depends on the length and criticality of the assignment and whether the work is sold directly to the customer or through a consultancy.
